# Plexo Rebrand V2 -- Phase 1 Personality Brief

**Date:** 2026-04-24
**Depends on:** 0-AUDIT.md

---

## One-Sentence Personality

Plexo is the dense, electric substrate that engineers wire their intelligence into -- infrastructure-grade, always running, never performing.

---

## 3 Reference Brands

**1. Datadog (Observability Platform)**
Systems product that earns trust through density. Dark canvas, saturated signal colors, data-forward layouts. No hand-holding, no empty states begging you to "get started." The UI assumes competence. Plexo should feel like this: a surface that rewards expertise.

**2. PostgreSQL (Database)**
Decades of credibility from doing one thing with total rigor. The brand is the reliability. No trend-chasing, no redesigns for redesign's sake. Postgres doesn't need to look exciting -- it IS exciting to the people who matter. Plexo's brand should carry this weight: the product earns the identity, not the other way around.

**3. Cloudflare Workers (Edge Runtime)**
Developer infrastructure with genuine ambition. Dark-mode defaults, monospace-forward, config-as-interface. Workers feels like a tool built by the people who use it. Not a dashboard pretending to be simple -- a dashboard that respects the complexity it manages.

---

## 3 Anti-References

**1. Vercel Dashboard**
Beautiful. Empty. The entire brand is negative space and status indicators. Plexo manages agents, memory, semantic cognition layers, task orchestration -- there is no version of Plexo that can afford to be sparse. Vercel-aesthetic would make Plexo feel like a toy wrapping serious machinery.

**2. Cursor**
AI-product-as-lifestyle-brand. Soft gradients, rounded everything, the implication that AI is gentle and approachable. Plexo is not approachable in that way. Plexo is a systems platform. Its users don't want to feel cozy -- they want to feel in control.

**3. Generic AI Startup (Jasper, Copy.ai, etc.)**
Purple gradients, "magic" language, friendly illustrations, onboarding carousels. These brands exist to make non-technical users comfortable with AI. Plexo's audience already knows what AI is. They want to configure it, not be introduced to it.

## Mood Vocabulary

- **Substrate** -- underlying layer, not surface decoration
- **Wired** -- connected, electric, active signal
- **Dense** -- information-rich, no wasted space
- **Precise** -- mathematical, not approximate
- **Persistent** -- always-on, stateful, remembering
- **Deep** -- layers below the surface, vertical not horizontal
- **Charged** -- energy without noise, potential without chaos
- **Nocturnal** -- late-night terminals, focused work, blue-shifted light

## Voice Spec

Plexo speaks like system documentation written by someone who respects your time -- terse, accurate, zero theater.

---

## What Plexo Is NOT

- Not a chatbot wrapper. It has chat, but chat is one input surface among many.
- Not a no-code platform. Configuration is code-adjacent (SCL, extensions, model routing rules).
- Not friendly-first. It is clear-first. Friendliness is a side effect of good information design.
- Not a dashboard. It is a control plane.
- Not neutral. It has opinions about how agent systems should work and it enforces them.
- Not ephemeral. Sessions have memory, agents persist, context accumulates. The opposite of disposable.

---

## Directional Pulls

**Surface:** Deep canvas with layered depth. Cards emerge from the background, they don't float above it. Think geological strata, not Material Design elevation. Surfaces are carved out, not stacked on.

**Accent:** High-saturation signal against low-saturation ground. The accent should feel like voltage -- visible, immediate, purposeful. Never decorative. Every accent pixel means something is alive or actionable.

**Type:** Monospace presence throughout, not just in code blocks. Display type should feel engineered, not designed -- geometric sans with tight tracking. Body type prioritizes scan speed at small sizes. Numerals always tabular.

**Layout:** Dense defaults. Information grids over hero sections. Vertical rhythm driven by data cadence, not visual breathing room. Sidebars that carry weight. Tables that don't apologize for having rows.

**Motion:** State transitions only. No entrance animations for their own sake. Loading states that communicate work being done (the Delta Frame breathing). Hover states that reveal information, not decoration. Motion budget: near zero for cosmetic, generous for functional.

## Distinctiveness Commitments

### vs. Joeybuilt (Parent Brand)

Joeybuilt is the house. Plexo is the engine room. Joeybuilt can be warm, approachable, human-forward. Plexo runs hotter. Where Joeybuilt uses the family Geist display font for friendly authority, Plexo uses it tighter, smaller, more utilitarian. Plexo's accent runs cooler and more saturated than any sibling. The Delta Frame symbol belongs only to Plexo -- no other Joeybuilt product uses an open geometric mark. Plexo always carries the `_plexo` underscore prefix, signaling its infrastructure nature.

### vs. Levio (Sibling -- Client App)

Levio is a Plexo client. It should feel like an application built ON Plexo, not like Plexo itself. Where Plexo shows the wiring, Levio hides it. Where Plexo is dense, Levio is focused. If Levio is the cockpit, Plexo is the avionics bay. They share family DNA (fonts, radius, no-shadow rule) but diverge on density, accent saturation, and information architecture.

### vs. Other Siblings (Frame Forge, Hub, Future Products)

Every Joeybuilt product shares the skeleton (Geist, JetBrains Mono, 4px radius, no shadows). Plexo differentiates through: (1) highest information density of any sibling, (2) most saturated accent, (3) monospace-heaviest UI, (4) the only product with a semantic concept graph as a core surface, (5) the only product where the brand mark animates with system state.

## Panel Sign-Off

**Mira Castellan (Symbol & Mark)**
The Delta Frame is strong. Open triangle with vertex dots reads as "network node" and "incomplete-by-design" simultaneously -- correct for a platform that users complete by wiring in their own agents and models. The breathing animation tied to agent state is the single best brand decision from V1. Preserve it. The `_plexo` underscore wordmark should be non-negotiable -- it is the typographic equivalent of the open triangle: a system identifier, not a product name.

**Rohan Devereaux (Systems Aesthetic)**
The directional pull toward Datadog/Postgres density is correct, but guard against "dark theme = systems product" laziness. The systems aesthetic must come from information architecture, not just color. Dense layouts, tabular data, monospace presence, config-as-interface patterns -- these are what make it feel like infrastructure. A dark theme alone is just a preference toggle.

**Olu Ade (Typography)**
Kill the font loading mismatch immediately -- it undermines every other typographic decision. Geist for display, IBM Plex Sans for body, JetBrains Mono for code. This is a strong stack. The key V2 move: push monospace further into the UI. Status labels, numerical displays, breadcrumbs, timestamps -- anywhere precision matters, JetBrains Mono. Tabular numerals everywhere, no exceptions.

**Yuki Renaud (Color)**
The current accent at hue 193 is correct territory -- cool, technical, not corporate blue. V2 should increase saturation by 15-20% in dark mode. The accent must feel electric, not steel. Surface palette is good but needs one more step of contrast between canvas and surface-1. Purge every hardcoded hex. Signal colors (amber, red) stay semantic -- never use them decoratively. Add a green for success/active states. No gradients. None.

**Dr. Liesel Marchand (Semiotics)**
Plexo's semiotic challenge: it is a platform (generic, substrate) that must feel specific and opinionated. The Delta Frame solves this at the symbol level -- geometric, precise, intentionally incomplete. The danger is the rest of the brand defaulting to "generic dark dashboard." Every surface should encode Plexo's specific concepts: memory is persistent (not ephemeral chat), agents are autonomous (not assistants), cognition has structure (SCL regions, attractors). The concept graph is not a feature -- it is the brand's central metaphor made visible.

**Marcus Pell (Anti-Clone)**
Run the clone test: screenshot Plexo, remove the logo, show it to ten engineers. If more than two guess "Datadog" or "Grafana," the density direction went too far into homage. Plexo's differentiator is the semantic layer -- SCL concept graphs, attractor browsers, region maps. No monitoring tool has these. Lean into the surfaces that only Plexo can have. The concept graph should be as recognizable as Figma's canvas or VS Code's sidebar. That is the anti-clone commitment.
